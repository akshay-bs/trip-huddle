# Destination Brief

## At a glance
- Two states in north-east India, reached through Guwahati: the plains and river of Assam, and the green Khasi and Jaintia hills of Meghalaya.
- Meghalaya is waterfalls, caves, living root bridges, clean villages and a clear river at the border. Assam is wildlife (one-horned rhinos), tea gardens, temples and the Brahmaputra.
- It suits groups who like short walks, viewpoints and nature more than nightlife. Hill roads are slow, so the days are shorter than the map suggests.

## Safety and rules
- **Safety:** most visitors have trouble-free trips. Read your own government's advice for India and its north-east before booking: some border districts of the region have their own warnings, and they change (UK FCDO, India travel advice, checked 2026-11-03).
- **Visa:** visitors from outside India need an Indian visa; many nationalities can apply online for an e-Visa (Indian e-Visa portal, checked 2026-11-03). Indian citizens need nothing for Assam or Meghalaya.
- **Permits:** no permit is needed for the usual places in Assam and Meghalaya. Check the state tourism site for any visitor registration rule before you go (Meghalaya Tourism, checked 2026-11-03).
- **Health:** drink bottled or filtered water, and ask a travel clinic about vaccines for India (NHS Fit for Travel, India page, checked 2026-11-03).
- **Money:** Indian rupees (₹). ATMs and cards work in Guwahati and Shillong; carry cash for villages, parking, guides and small entry fees (Wikivoyage, Meghalaya, checked 2026-11-03).
- **Customs:** much of Meghalaya is Christian, and Christmas week is busy in Shillong. Ask before taking photos of people, and keep to marked paths in sacred forests.

## When to go
- **October to April** is the dry season: clear views, easy roads. December is cool (Shillong and Sohra nights around 4–8 °C), with morning fog in Sohra and early sunsets, around 4:30 pm.
- **Waterfalls** run strongest from June to September, in the monsoon, when Sohra is one of the wettest places on earth and some roads and trails close.
- **Kaziranga** is open to visitors from about November to April, and closed in the monsoon (Kaziranga National Park, checked 2026-11-03).
- **Crowds:** Christmas and New Year fill Shillong and Sohra; book stays early for those weeks.

## How long
- Usual trips are **6 to 12 days**.
- **Short (5–6 days):** Shillong, Sohra and a day to Dawki and Mawlynnong.
- **Usual (8–10 days):** the same, plus 2 nights at Kaziranga.
- **Long (12 days or more):** add Majuli or the Jaintia hills around Jowai.

## Kinds of experiences
- **Waterfalls and viewpoints:** Sohra, the Khasi hills, the Jaintia hills.
- **Living root bridges and treks:** the valleys below Sohra, and around Mawlynnong.
- **Caves:** Sohra, and the Jaintia hills.
- **River and border:** Dawki and Shnongpdeng, on the Umngot river.
- **Wildlife:** Kaziranga and Pobitora, in Assam.
- **Tea, temples and culture:** the tea gardens of Assam, the temples of Guwahati, the river island of Majuli.

## Regions
- **Guwahati (Assam):** the gateway, with the airport and temples. Fine in December.
- **Kaziranga and central Assam:** rhinos and tea gardens. December is a good time.
- **Majuli and upper Assam:** a quiet river island. Far from the rest; needs 2 nights or more.
- **Shillong (East Khasi hills):** the hill capital, the best base for evenings out. Cold nights in December.
- **Sohra (Cherrapunji):** waterfalls, caves and root bridges. Thinner falls and morning fog in December.
- **Dawki and Mawlynnong (south border):** the clear river and the clean village. Best in the dry months.
- **Jowai (Jaintia hills):** big waterfalls and caves, on slow border roads. Plan to be off the roads before dark.

## Common routes
- Guwahati → Shillong (2N) → Sohra (2N) → Shillong (1N) → Kaziranga (2N) → Guwahati, from [[Example - Public research]].
- Guwahati → Kaziranga (2N) → Shillong (2N) → Sohra (2N) → Dawki (1N) → Guwahati, the same towns the other way round, from [[Example - Public research]].
- Guwahati → Shillong (3N) → Guwahati, with day trips to Sohra, Dawki and Mawlynnong, for a short trip, from [[Example - Public research]].

## Budget
Rough daily ranges per person, all in, in ₹ (estimates; stays and the vehicle are most of it):
- **Budget:** about 2,000–3,500 a day: guesthouses and homestays, shared taxis.
- **Mid-range:** about 4,000–8,000 a day: good hotels and resorts, a hired car or van with driver.
- **Comfort:** about 10,000 a day and more: the best resorts and a private vehicle throughout.
- **What drives the cost:** the vehicle and driver (charged per day), stays in Christmas week, and the Kaziranga jeep safaris and park fees.

## Getting around
- A **hired car or van with a driver** from Guwahati or Shillong is the usual choice for a group: hill roads are slow and narrow, and drivers know them.
- **Shared taxis** run between the towns, for budget travel; they fill before they leave.
- **No trains** reach the Meghalaya hills; Guwahati is the nearest railway station and the main airport.
- **Self-drive** cars are hard to find, and the hill roads are hard work after dark.
