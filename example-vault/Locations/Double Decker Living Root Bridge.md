# Double Decker Living Root Bridge

## Overview
The famous two-level living root bridge over the Umshiang River at Nongriat village, below Sohra (Cherrapunji) in East Khasi Hills. It was grown by the Khasi people from the roots of rubber fig trees, one bridge stacked above the other, and is thought to be over 200 years old. There are no roads to Nongriat: you reach it by walking down about 3,000–3,500 steps from Tyrna village, which is about 12 km from Sohra town.

## Why go
- **The bridge itself:** the best-known living root bridge in Meghalaya and the only well-known double-decker. Blue-green pools below the bridge are good for a cold dip.
- **The walk:** two swaying steel-wire suspension bridges over rivers, other root bridges on the way, and jungle valley views.
- **Teens:** a real adventure trek, a swim in the pools and, for the fit, an extra hike to Rainbow Falls.
- **Adults:** a bucket-list sight, but only for those who can climb back up thousands of steps.

## Season reality
The best season for the trek. The steps are dry, the air is cool (comfortable for climbing) and the pools under the bridge are at their clearest. Rainbow Falls and the streams carry less water than after the monsoon, but the rainbow over the pool still appears on sunny late mornings. It is one of Meghalaya's busiest spots, so expect queues at the bridges on weekends and in the 24 Dec – 1 Jan week. With sunset around 4:30 pm, start early and start back up by about 1 pm.

## Access & walk
- **Walk:** about 3 km and 3,000–3,500 steps each way. Typical times are 1.5–2 hrs down and 2–3 hrs back up, so 4–6 hrs round trip plus time at the bridge. Add 2 hrs or so for Rainbow Falls (about 1 hr further from the bridge).
- **Surface / steps / railings:** concrete steps with railings in many places; two steel-wire suspension bridges. Steep and relentless in both directions.
- **Slippery or exposed sections:** the suspension bridges sway; wet rocks around the pools.
- **Can part of the group skip it and wait?** Yes, at Tyrna, but there is little to do there. A better split is for non-trekkers to visit another Sohra sight with the vehicle while the trekkers go down.
- **Last-mile road / vehicle access:** paved road from Sohra to Tyrna parking; tempo traveller OK.

## Activities
- Walk across both levels of the bridge
- Swim or paddle in the natural pools below the bridge (cold in December)
- Cross the two suspension bridges on the way
- Extra hike to [[Blue Lagoon (Nongriat)]] (about 30 min) or [[Rainbow Falls]] (about 1–1.5 hrs each way from the bridge; fit members only)
- Stay a night in Nongriat at a basic homestay (splits the climb across two days)

## Tips
- Not a match for the group's "no long treks" rule. If kept, make it optional for the teens and keen adults, and plan the rest of that day for everyone else.
- Wear shoes with grip and carry water, snacks, a towel and a change of clothes if swimming. Shops on the way sell water and snacks.
- Carry cash for fees, snacks and a guide.
- Anyone with knee problems should not attempt it; the descent is as hard on knees as the climb is on lungs.
- Easier alternative: [[Riwai Living Root Bridge]] near Mawlynnong is a short walk and gives most of the root-bridge experience. [[Rangthylliang Living Root Bridge]] is another option already on the list.
- Best time, as first written: Start from Tyrna by 8 am; begin the climb back by 1 pm to be out before dark (sunset about 4:30 pm)

## Nearby / combine with
- Further along the same trail: [[Blue Lagoon (Nongriat)]] (about 30 min) and [[Rainbow Falls]] (about 1–1.5 hrs each way).
- Two Cherrapunjee stays on the list, Royal View Resort and Smoky Falls Mae Fi Resort, are in Tyrna at the trailhead. An early start from there saves the 30–40 min drive.
- Other Sohra sights for the rest of the group that day: [[Nohkalikai Falls]], [[Mawsmai Cave]], [[Seven Sisters Falls]], [[Arwah Cave]].
