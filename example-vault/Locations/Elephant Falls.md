# Elephant Falls

## Overview
A stepped waterfall in Upper Shillong, about 12 km from the city centre, just off the road towards Sohra. Its Khasi name, *Ka Kshaid Lai Pateng Khohsiew*, means "three-step waterfall". The British named it after an elephant-shaped rock beside the falls. That rock was reportedly destroyed in the 1897 earthquake.

## Why go
- It is the classic Shillong city-tour waterfall. You see three falls stacked one below the other along a single staircase path, set among mossy rocks and ferns.
- **Teens:** photo spots at each tier, Khasi costume photo booths, souvenir stalls.
- **Adults:** the first tier needs almost no effort. Anyone can go as far down as they are comfortable.

## Season reality
December is the dry season, so the falls are **thin**. Water still flows, but it is a trickle compared with the monsoon photos. The lower pool is usually clear and green. It is pleasant in the sun and chilly in the shade. It is very busy with city-tour groups around Christmas, so go in the morning if possible. It is worth a quick stop because it is on the way, but it is not a highlight in December.

## Access & walk
- **Walk:** concrete stairs lead down past the tiers. The first fall is almost at the gate. The lowest (third) fall is about 100–150 steps down, depending on the source. Allow 30–45 min round trip with photo stops.
- **Surface / steps / railings:** well-built concrete steps with railings and viewing platforms.
- **Slippery or exposed sections:** steps near the water can be damp and mossy. Wear shoes with grip.
- **Can part of the group skip it and wait?** Yes. Anyone can stop at the first or second tier, or wait near the entrance stalls. The climb back up is the only tiring part.
- **Last-mile road / vehicle access:** paved road to the parking at the gate. A tempo traveller can reach it.

## Activities
- Walk down past the three tiers, with photos at each platform
- Khasi traditional costume photos (paid, optional)
- Souvenir and snack stalls at the entrance

## Tips
- **The fee is uncertain.** Older guides list ₹20 per person, while 2024–25 blogs and reviews report ₹100 per person plus parking.
- Swimming and climbing on the rocks are not allowed. Stay on the paths.
- Holidify lists it as closed on Sundays, but other sources say it is open daily. Treat this as unverified.
- Combine it with Shillong Peak in the same Upper Shillong loop.
- Best time, as first written: Morning to early afternoon (sun on the falls, fewer tour groups)
