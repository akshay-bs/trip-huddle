# Kamakhya Temple

## Overview
Kamakhya is one of the most important Shakti Peethas in India. It sits on Nilachal Hill in west Guwahati, about 8 km from the city centre and about 20 km from the airport. The present beehive-domed temple dates from the 16th–17th century and has a cave-like inner sanctum with no idol.

## Why go
- For adults, it is a major pilgrimage. Darshan here is the main reason for the visit.
- For everyone, the hilltop gives views over Guwahati and the Brahmaputra, and the temple architecture, sculpted panels and subsidiary shrines are interesting.
- For teens, it is less engaging. The long queue is the main downside. The courtyard, the architecture and the views are the interesting parts.

## Season reality
- The weather is ideal: cool and dry, 12–24 °C.
- Crowds are the problem.
  - The general queue commonly runs 3–5 hrs (longer on weekends and holidays).
  - A VIP pass typically cuts this to under 1 hr on normal days, but guides report 1–2+ hrs on holidays.
  - VIP passes are limited, so buy them online in advance.
- The temple closes 1:00–2:30 PM, so a late-morning start risks the queue being paused.
- Non-devotees can just see the complex and the views in about 45 min.

## Access & walk
- **Walk:** a few hundred metres up a sloping, busy lane from the parking or drop point to the gate. After that, you stand in the queue through covered corridors and go down steep, narrow steps into the dark sanctum and back out. Allow 20–30 min of actual walking. The queue time comes on top of that.
- **Surface / steps / railings:** stone and concrete paths. The steps into the sanctum are steep, uneven and wet, with handholds in places. There are ramps at the main entrance, and wheelchairs are reportedly available at Gate 4, but a wheelchair cannot enter the sanctum.
- **Slippery or exposed sections:** the sanctum floor and steps are wet (spring water) and slippery when barefoot.
- **Can part of the group skip it and wait?** Yes. Non-darshan members can walk around the courtyard or wait at the parking or eateries.
- **Last-mile road / vehicle access:** a hill road from Kamakhya Gate on AT Road/NH-27. Cars and taxis normally reach the hilltop parking. Large buses (and possibly tempo travellers on rush days) are held at foothill parking, with shared autos or shuttles going up.

## Activities
- Darshan in the garbhagriha (general free queue or VIP pass)
- Walk around the temple complex and the other Mahavidya shrines on Nilachal Hill (Bhuvaneshwari temple at the top has good city views)
- Photos of the exterior architecture and the view over Guwahati

## Tips
- **VIP pass:** ₹501 per person. **Since 15 Jun 2026, special darshan is booked online only**. Book as soon as the window opens; counter sales have stopped. Large vehicles may be stopped at the foot of Nilachal Hill, with shuttles up. For a big group that is about ₹6,500. Some guides also mention a costlier "VVIP" or special-puja option, but that is unverified.
- **Queue:** be at the gate by about 5:00–5:30 AM.
- **Dress code:** modest traditional clothes (saree, salwar kameez, kurta, full-length trousers). No shorts or sleeveless tops, and avoid leather belts and wallets inside.
- **Footwear:** remove it outside and use the shoe counters (token system). Carry socks, because the stone floor is cold in December.
- **Phones and cameras:** not allowed inside the sanctum. Deposit them at the counters (small fee) or leave them in the vehicle. Photos are allowed in the outer courtyard.
- Beware of touts offering "quick darshan" or pujas. Buy passes only from the official counter or website.
- Pigeons and goats roam the complex, and animal sacrifice happens on some days, so warn the teens.
- Best time, as first written: At opening (5:30 AM) or early morning on a weekday. Avoid weekends and holidays
