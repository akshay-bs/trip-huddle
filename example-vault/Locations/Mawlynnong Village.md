# Mawlynnong Village

## Overview
Mawlynnong is a small Khasi village of a few hundred people in the Pynursla block of East Khasi Hills, close to the Bangladesh border. It is about 90 km from Shillong and 30–35 km from Dawki. *Discover India* magazine called it "Asia's cleanest village" in 2003, and it has been a tourist stop ever since. It has flower-lined lanes, bamboo dustbins, thatch and tin houses, and the Church of the Epiphany. [[Riwai Living Root Bridge]] and [[Balancing Rock (Mawlynnong)]] are both within about 2–3 km.

## Why go
- **Adults:** a relaxed, flat stroll through tidy lanes and gardens, the old Anglican church, and a feel for Khasi village life and its community cleanliness rules. It is also a pleasant place to sleep between Sohra and Dawki.
- **Teens:** the Sky View bamboo platform (a climb above the treetops with a view towards the Bangladesh plains) and photos. There isn't much else to "do" here, so keep expectations modest.
- Some recent reviews call it overrated or commercialised when it is packed at midday. It is best early in the morning or on an overnight stay.

## December reality
- Dry, clear weather that is good for walking. Days are mild and nights are cold (8–18 °C). The hedges and flowers are less lush than just after the monsoon.
- It is the peak visiting season. Tour vehicles arrive around 11 AM – 3 PM, so the lanes and car park get busy and the Sky View has a queue.
- Christmas: Mawlynnong is a church-going Christian village. On Sundays and around 24–25 Dec, expect services, closed stalls and limited homestay kitchens.
- Verdict: good. Worth it as a relaxed village walk combined with the root bridge. Don't treat it as a highlight on its own.

## Access & walk
- **Walk:** about 1–1.5 km of loose looping through the village lanes, taking 45–60 min at an easy pace, plus about 15–20 min for the Sky View if you climb it.
- **Surface / steps / railings:** paved or stone-flagged lanes, mostly level with gentle slopes. The Sky View is a bamboo structure of ladders and stairs tied to trees. It sways slightly and has bamboo railings.
- **Slippery or exposed sections:** the bamboo steps can be slippery with morning dew. The Sky View isn't for anyone uncomfortable with heights.
- **Can part of the group skip it and wait?** Yes. The walk is optional in parts, and anyone can sit at a homestay café or near the car park. The Sky View is fully optional.
- **Last-mile road / vehicle access:** paved road to a car park at the village entrance. There is no driving inside the village. The tempo traveller is fine.

## Activities
- Village walk: lanes, gardens, bamboo dustbins and the Church of the Epiphany
- **Sky View / "Bangladesh view"**: a bamboo machan (platform) built around trees, about 80–85 ft up, with a view over the village towards the Bangladesh plains. It costs about ₹10–30 per person. Don't confuse it with the separate **Nohwet Sky Walk / Nohwet View Point** on the Riwai side, which is also a tall bamboo structure. Ask the driver which one you are going to.
- Small handicraft and snack stalls (bamboo items, local honey, tea)
- Overnight homestay, the best way to see the village quiet in the evening and early morning
- Combine with [[Riwai Living Root Bridge]] and [[Balancing Rock (Mawlynnong)]] (both 5–10 min drive)

## Tips
- Keep to the rules: no littering (use the bamboo bins), no smoking in lanes, and ask before photographing people or homes.
- Carry cash for entry, parking, the Sky View and toilets. The mobile network and UPI can be patchy.
- Homestays are simple (roughly ₹1,500–3,000 per room, often with meals) and small. a big group will usually need 2–3 homestays or a larger guesthouse. Book early for the week before Christmas.
- Arrive before 10 AM, or after 3:30 PM if staying the night, to avoid the day-tour rush.
- Fees here change often and sources disagree, so confirm at the gate.
