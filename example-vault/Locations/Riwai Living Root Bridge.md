# Riwai Living Root Bridge

## Overview
This is a single-decker living root bridge over a small forest stream at Riwai, next to Nohwet and 2–3 km from Mawlynnong in East Khasi Hills. OpenStreetMap and Tripadvisor also call it the **Jingmaham (Zingmaham) Living Root Bridge**. Khasi villagers made it by training the aerial roots of a rubber fig (*Ficus elastica*) across the stream over decades, and it is still in everyday use. Of all Meghalaya's root bridges it takes the least effort to reach, so it is the one most tour itineraries use.

## Why go
- It's the "living root bridge" experience without a trek. You walk down a short wooded path, cross the bridge and sit by the stream.
- Adults: a genuine example of indigenous bio-engineering, a UNESCO tentative-list theme ("Jingkieng Jri" living root bridge cultural landscapes).
- Teens: walking on a bridge that is a living tree, and photos from the stream bed below.
- **Contrast:** the famous **Nongriat double-decker** ([[Double Decker Living Root Bridge]]) near Sohra needs roughly 3,000–3,500 steps down from Tyrna and back up again. That is about 4–6 hrs round trip on steep steps. It is in neither itinerary and is not suitable for this group. Riwai gives most of the experience for about 5% of the effort.

## December reality
- Dry and firm underfoot, which is the safest time of year for the steps. The stream is low and clear.
- Peak season crowds: the bridge is short (about 20–25 m, single file), so at midday you may wait for a photo gap.
- Verdict: good. It is easy and worthwhile, especially early in the morning. Don't expect the monsoon drama of photos taken in August.

## Access & walk
- **Walk:** about 300 m each way from the car park, roughly 0.6 km and 30 min round trip plus time at the bridge. Allow 1 hr in all.
- **Surface / steps / railings:** a paved and stone-stepped path through forest. Sources give anything from 40–60 to about 150 steps each way, depending on which entrance they used and what they counted. The easier Riwai/Nohwet entrance is the one tours use. There are some railings on the path, and the bridge itself has root "handrails".
- **Slippery or exposed sections:** the roots on the bridge and the rocks at the stream can be slick, even in the dry season. Wear shoes with grip. There are no big drops.
- **Can part of the group skip it and wait?** Yes, at the car park stalls. The climb back up is the only effort, and most adults will manage it with a pause or two.
- **Last-mile road / vehicle access:** a narrow paved road to a small village car park. The tempo traveller is OK but may have to wait or park on the road edge at peak time.

## Activities
- Walk across the bridge and down to the stream bed for the classic photo from below
- Short stop at the car park stalls (local fruit, tea, bamboo crafts)
- [[Balancing Rock (Mawlynnong)]] and the Nohwet View Point / Sky Walk are close by and usually visited in the same loop

## Tips
- Go first thing (before about 10:30 AM).
- Wear closed shoes with grip, not sandals.
- One-way foot traffic on the bridge at busy times. Be patient and keep groups small on the bridge.
- Carry cash for entry, camera and parking.
- Don't pull or stand on young roots, and don't carve anything. The bridge is alive.
