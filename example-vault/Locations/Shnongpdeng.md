# Shnongpdeng

## Overview
Shnongpdeng is a small village on the Umngot River about 8 km upstream of Dawki, and around 90 km (3–3.5 hrs) from Shillong. It is where most of the Umngot adventure activities happen: scuba try-dives, snorkelling, kayaking, a zipline across the river, cliff jumping, boat rides and riverside camping. There are dozens of camps along a pebbly beach, and a pedestrian suspension bridge crosses the river. It is quieter than the Dawki boat ghat but still busy in peak season.

## Why go
- **Teens:** the best "adventure" stop of the trip. A try-dive or snorkel in clear river water, a zipline over the river, kayaking and (for the brave) cliff jumps.
- **Adults:** a traditional boat ride or kayak on the same clear water as Dawki with fewer boats around, plus the suspension bridge, the riverbank and tea at a camp. Nobody has to do anything strenuous.
- It is a better place than Dawki ghat to spend a few hours. Dawki is mainly for the classic boat photo and the border view.

## Season reality
- **Water clarity:** normally excellent from November to February. However, in October–November 2025 the Umngot turned murky from Shillong–Dawki highway construction silt, and Meghalaya's pollution board flagged debris dumping. Clarity was expected to return by late November. Road work continues into 2026, so check recent Instagram or Google photos in early December 2026 before paying for scuba or snorkelling.
- **Cold:** days are about 10–22 °C, and the river water is cold. No reliable measured water temperature was found. Scuba operators supply wetsuits. Snorkelling and cliff jumps will feel chilly, so bring a towel and warm dry clothes. Morning mist can lie over the river.
- **Crowds:** weekends, Christmas and New Year are the busiest. Picnic groups, music and full camps are common.
- Verdict: good. It is excellent for the teens if the water is clear, but book scuba slots ahead and avoid Christmas Eve if possible.

## Access & walk
- **Walk:** from the parking down to the riverbank camps and back is roughly 0.5–1 km in all. Allow about 30 min of walking in total, including the suspension bridge.
- **Surface / steps / railings:** a concrete path or steps down to the river, then pebbles and sand on the bank. The suspension bridge is a narrow steel-and-plank footbridge that sways. The exact number of steps down to the river wasn't found in any source. Descriptions suggest a few minutes' descent, which is harder coming back up.
- **Slippery or exposed sections:** wet rocks at the water's edge and the cliff-jump rocks. The suspension bridge moves, which some people dislike.
- **Can part of the group skip it and wait?** Yes. Non-participants can sit at a camp with tea while the teens do activities.
- **Last-mile road / vehicle access:** a narrow, winding village road from the highway about 8 km before Dawki. The 17-seater can get there, but passing is tight and parking fills up at peak times.

- **Operators seen online:** Travenjo, Universal Adventures, Pioneer Adventure Tour (local, runs Pioneer Camp), Kajaawa. Many riverbank camps (Pioneer, Frank Riverside, Bob's, Laiva, Marvel's and others on OpenStreetMap) sell activities directly.
- **For a big group:** a realistic mix is scuba for the teens and any keen adults (₹3,200–3,500 each), with everyone else doing a boat ride and kayak. Budget roughly ₹25,000–35,000 for the group.

## Tips
- Book scuba 1–2 weeks ahead for Christmas week, and confirm the operator runs try-dives on your date. Dives stop when the water is murky or high.
- The 14-year-old is eligible for a try-dive (minimum age 10). A parent must sign the consent form.
- Carry cash. The network is weak here, and there are no ATMs at Shnongpdeng.
- Bring towels, a change of warm clothes and sandals for the pebbly bank.
- Cliff jumping from about 8 m or 14 m into cold water is a real risk. Consider limiting it to the lowest point, or skip it.
- Scuba (3–4 hrs) plus Dawki boating plus that drive is a very long day.
- Best time, as first written: Morning (9 AM – 1 PM). Scuba slots usually run about 10 AM – 1 PM, the light on the water is best before early afternoon, and it is less crowded
