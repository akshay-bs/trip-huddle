# Umananda Temple

## Overview
A Shiva temple on Umananda (Peacock) Island in the middle of the Brahmaputra at Guwahati, often called one of the world's smallest inhabited river islands. The temple was first built in 1694 under the Ahom king Gadadhar Singha and rebuilt after later earthquake damage. You reach it by a short ferry ride from Umananda/Kachari Ghat on MG Road.

## Why go
- The ferry ride across the Brahmaputra is the fun part: views of the river, city skyline and Saraighat bridge.
- **Teens:** boat ride, island setting, possibly monkeys (the island is known for langurs, though few remain).
- **Adults:** an important Shiva shrine with carvings, and a peaceful spot mid-river.

## Season reality
Low, calm water and clear weather make the crossing easy. The main constraint is the **government ferry schedule** (roughly 9 AM – 2 PM), so plan it for the morning of the city-tour day. Private boats run later but cost more.

## Access & walk
- **Walk:** short, but with a steep stone staircase from the landing up to the temple.
- **Surface / steps / railings:** stone steps, some uneven; partial railings.
- **Slippery or exposed sections:** boarding and leaving the ferry by gangplank can be wobbly; steps can be wet near the water.
- **Can part of the group skip it and wait?** Yes, at the ghat, or stay by the riverfront.
- **Last-mile road / vehicle access:** drop at the ghat on MG Road; the vehicle waits nearby.

## Activities
- Ferry across the Brahmaputra and back
- Temple darshan; walk around the small island
- Alternative view: the Guwahati passenger ropeway passes over the island on its way to North Guwahati

## Tips
- Check the latest ferry times on the day (the Assam Inland Water Transport app or at the ghat).
- For a big group, a private boat for the whole group may be simpler than waiting for the government ferry.
- Dress modestly; remove shoes at the temple.
- Best time, as first written: Morning (9–11 AM), to catch government ferries both ways
