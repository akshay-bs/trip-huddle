# Umngot River (Dawki)

## Overview
The Umngot (also called Wah Umngot or the Dawki River) flows out of the Khasi–Jaintia hills past the border town of Dawki in West Jaintia Hills and into Bangladesh, where it becomes the Piyain at Jaflong. It is often called one of India's clearest rivers. In the dry season the riverbed is visible several metres down, so boats look as if they are floating in the air. The main boating ghat is on the Dawki bank just downstream of the **Dawki suspension bridge**, which the British built in 1932 and which still carries the highway. Dawki is about 80–90 km (3 hrs or more) from Shillong and about 30–35 km from Mawlynnong.

## Why go
- The signature Meghalaya photo: a wooden boat on glass-clear green water with the Dawki bridge behind.
- Everyone can do it. The boat ride needs no fitness, swimming or walking to speak of.
- The India–Bangladesh border is right there. The Bangladeshi bank and Jaflong's stone-collecting boats are visible from the water, and the "white water" line marks the unofficial border.
- Teens: the calmer, more active version (kayak, snorkel, scuba, zipline) is 8 km upstream at [[Shnongpdeng]].

## Season reality
- **Clarity:** normally at its best from November to February. **Caveat:** in October–November 2025, hill-cutting and debris from the Shillong–Dawki highway widening turned the river brown for weeks, and the Meghalaya pollution board flagged violations. It was expected to clear from late November. The road works are still going on in 2026, so check recent Google Maps or Instagram photos in early December 2026. Some visitors in winter 2025–26 still found murky water near the busy jetties, but it was clearer upstream.
- **Crowds:** Christmas to New Year is the busiest week of the year. Expect long queues at the boat counter, dozens of boats jostling at the ghat (which spoils the "lonely boat" photo), and a crowded bridge.
- **Traffic:** the approach road is narrow and shared with long lines of trucks carrying limestone and coal to the Bangladesh land port, and the old single-lane bridge is a bottleneck. Highway widening brings sudden stoppages. Reports describe vehicles stranded for hours during road-work blockades, and long tourist jams in peak season. No hard figures were found, so plan a buffer of 1–2 hrs. Weekdays have *more* truck traffic, while weekends and holidays have more tourists.
- That is the worst combination: a jam at Dawki can wreck the whole day.
- Verdict: good. It is worth it for the visual, but go early (be at the ghat by 7:30–8 AM) and not on Christmas Eve if you can avoid it.

## Access & walk
- **Walk:** from the riverside car park below the bridge to the boats is about 200 m, around 15 min round trip including getting in and out of the boat.
- **Surface / steps / railings:** concrete steps and a slope down to a stony riverbank. Stepping into the narrow wooden boats needs a hand from the boatman.
- **Slippery or exposed sections:** wet rocks at the waterline. The boats are low and tippy, so sit still and keep life jackets on.
- **Can part of the group skip it and wait?** Yes. Anyone can wait at the ghat stalls or view it from the bridge.
- **Last-mile road / vehicle access:** directly on the highway. The tempo traveller can use the riverside parking, but the driver will know when to drop you at the bridge and park elsewhere on jam days.

## Activities
- **Boat ride** from the Dawki ghat: about 30–50 min upstream and back, from about ₹800 per boat (3–4 people). For a big group book **4 boats**. Pay at the cooperative counter, not to touts.
- View from the **Dawki bridge** (1932 suspension bridge). This is the classic top-down photo, when traffic allows.
- Look at the border and the Bangladesh bank. The Dawki–Tamabil land port is about 1 km away. Don't cross or wander into the border area.
- Water sports (kayak, snorkel, scuba try-dive, zipline, cliff jump, camping) at [[Shnongpdeng]], 8 km upstream.

## Tips
- Be at the ghat by 7:30–8 AM. Staying the night at Mawlynnong (both itineraries) makes this possible, since it is about 1 hr away.
- Use the official counter. Rates are per boat, so confirm the price, the duration and how many people per boat before boarding.
- Carry cash. There is no reliable ATM or network at the ghat.
- Choose Dawki *or* Shnongpdeng for the long boat ride. Dawki has the bridge and border view, while Shnongpdeng is calmer and has the activities. With a long drive afterwards, a short Dawki ride plus a photo from the bridge may be enough.
- Before travelling, ask the driver or hotel about road-work closures on the Shillong–Dawki highway. Stoppages are sometimes announced at short notice.
- Don't swim at the Dawki ghat. The current and boat traffic make it unsafe. Swim only in designated areas at Shnongpdeng with a life jacket.
- Best time, as first written: Early morning (7–10 AM) for fewer boats, a calmer road and good light. Late morning sun overhead gives the strongest see-through effect
