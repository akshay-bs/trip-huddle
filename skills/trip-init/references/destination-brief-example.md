# Destination Brief

**Checked on 2026-11-03.** Safety, visa, health and money rules change. Check the official advice again before you book.

## At a glance
- A small, sunny country at the western edge of Europe: a hilly capital by a river, wide plains of cork oaks and vineyards inland, and a south coast of cliffs, coves and long beaches.
- Easy for a first trip: good roads, short distances, food and wine at fair prices.
- It suits groups who want towns, beaches and food more than hard walking, though old town centres are steep and cobbled.

## Safety and rules
- **Safety:** a safe country to visit. Watch for pickpockets on Lisbon's trams and at viewpoints, and for strong currents on Atlantic beaches; swim where there is a lifeguard flag (UK FCDO, Portugal travel advice, checked 2026-11-03).
- **Visa:** Portugal is in the Schengen area. Many visitors from outside the EU need no visa for up to 90 days in 180; non-EU visitors are recorded by the EU's Entry/Exit System at the border, and should check whether they need an ETIAS travel authorisation before booking (EU Travel to Europe site, checked 2026-11-03).
- **Health:** no special vaccines; tap water is safe to drink. June sun is strong: shade, water and sunscreen on beach days. EU visitors carry their EHIC (NHS Fit for Travel, Portugal page, checked 2026-11-03).
- **Money:** euros (€). Cards work almost everywhere; carry some cash for small cafes, markets and parking. ATMs are called Multibanco (Wikivoyage, Portugal, checked 2026-11-03).
- **Customs:** lunch is around 1 pm and dinner from 8 pm. Cover shoulders in churches.
- **Fires:** inland areas have wildfire warnings in hot, dry weeks. Follow local advice on closed roads and paths (UK FCDO, Portugal travel advice, checked 2026-11-03).

## When to go
- **May, June and September** are the best months: warm, long days, fewer crowds than July and August.
- **Your dates (early to mid June):** Lisbon is warm and busy with its June street festivals in the middle of the month, so book stays early. The Alentejo is hot in the afternoons (often 30 °C or more): plan visits for the morning and evening. The Algarve is sunny, with a cool sea (about 19–20 °C) and calm water in the coves (IPMA climate normals, checked 2026-11-03).
- Sunset is around 9 pm, so the days are long.

## How long
- Usual trips are **7 to 14 days**.
- **Short (5–7 days):** Lisbon and the Algarve.
- **Usual (9–10 days):** Lisbon, a stop in the Alentejo, and the Algarve, like your dates.
- **Long (14 days or more):** add Porto and the north, or the Douro valley.

## Kinds of experiences
- **Beaches and sea caves:** the Algarve, west to east.
- **Old towns and castles:** Lisbon, the walled hill towns of the Alentejo, and a few towns in the Algarve.
- **Wine:** the Alentejo, around Évora.
- **Viewpoints and cliffs:** Lisbon's hills, the Algarve's west coast.
- **Food:** grilled fish on the coast, pork and bread dishes inland, pastries everywhere.

## Regions
- **Lisbon:** the capital: old districts, river views, museums, day trips to palaces in the hills. Warm and busy in June.
- **Alentejo:** quiet plains, walled towns, wineries, Évora's old centre. Hot afternoons in June.
- **Algarve:** the south coast: beaches, coves, sea caves, cliff paths. Its best season starts in June.
- **Porto and the north** (not in your regions): river city, port wine, the Douro valley. Too far for this trip.

- Lisbon (3N) → Évora (2N) → Lagos (4N), from [[Example Blog - Ten days in the south]].

## Budget
Rough daily ranges per person, all in, in € (estimates; stays and the vehicle are most of it):
- **Budget:** about 60–90 a day: guesthouses, buses and trains, simple meals.
- **Mid-range:** about 120–200 a day: good hotels, a hired car or van, restaurant meals.
- **Comfort:** about 250 a day and more: the best hotels, a driver, boat trips and tastings.
- **What drives the cost:** stays in Lisbon and on the coast in summer, the hired vehicle and motorway tolls, and paid activities (boat trips, wine tastings).

## Getting around
- A **hired car or van** is the easy choice for a group of six: motorways link all three regions, and the coast has many small places buses don't reach. Most motorways have electronic tolls; ask the rental company for a toll device.
- **Trains** run from Lisbon to Évora and to the Algarve, and **express buses** link the towns: good for a group without a driver, but slower between beaches.
- Parking in Lisbon's old districts is hard: leave the vehicle at the hotel there.
